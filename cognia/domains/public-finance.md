# Domain: public finance

Load only when the source is in public finance or optimal taxation.

## Hard spots: automatic Tier 1 in this field

- **Small-reform (variational) arguments**: "raise the rate by dτ and add up the effects".
  Show one reform with numbers for a single taxpayer before any integral.
- **Elasticities of different things**: labour supply, taxable income, and the tax base are
  not the same elasticity. State which one, with respect to what (usually the net-of-tax
  rate 1 − τ, not τ), and why it matters.
- **Social welfare weights** (g, ḡ): explain them as a value judgment in money terms
  ("a dollar to this person is worth g dollars of public money") before any welfare function.
- **Mechanical versus behavioural effects**: draw them as separate pieces every time; most
  confusion comes from merging them.
- **Incidence**: who writes the cheque is not who bears the tax. Use a supply and demand
  wedge with numbers.

## Diagram vocabulary

- **Marginal rate schedule**: a step chart of rates by income, real brackets, the bracket
  under discussion in solid. Structural; good opening context.
- **Revenue (Laffer) curve**: revenue against the rate, peak marked. Illustrative; pair it
  with the gain-versus-loss crossing, which shows why the peak sits where it does.
- **Harberger triangle**: the deadweight-loss triangle between supply and demand under a tax.
  Illustrative; make the triangle grow with a slider.
- **Incidence wedge**: supply and demand with the tax as a vertical gap and both burdens
  shaded. Illustrative.
- **Pareto tail**: the density above a threshold with the mean above it marked.

## Common misreadings

- **"A higher elasticity means people work less."** Taxable income also responds through
  avoidance and timing, so a high taxable-income elasticity can reflect the tax base design.
- **"The revenue-maximising rate is the optimal rate."** Only when ḡ = 0; say so whenever
  the two coincide.

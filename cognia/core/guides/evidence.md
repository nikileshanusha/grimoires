# Guide: evidence and doubt

The aim is that "the authors argue X" never quietly becomes "X is true".

## Evidence tags

Tag every claim about a source, in the worksheet, on vault pages and in the explainer.

| Tag | Use for | Explainer mark |
|---|---|---|
| **Established** | what the source directly shows: a proof, a stated estimate, a measured result | solid square |
| **Derived** | follows from the source's own equations or results by your arithmetic, but is not stated in it | outlined square |
| **Interpretation** | a reasonable reading of what a result means, or a value judgment | dashed square |
| **External** | from outside the source; never let it look like the source's claim | dotted square |
| **Open** | the source does not settle it (out of scope, assumed, or a gap) | dotted circle |

## Central results: the falsifiability argument

For anything that answers the six questions in `/cognia:ingest`, work out the claim, its
evidence and tag, the assumptions that must hold, another explanation of the same evidence,
and what would show the claim false. On the page, write it as one short connected argument,
not a form: "The authors find X, and the evidence is Y. That only holds if Z. If Z failed,
the same data could come from W, and the design rules that out because…". Secondary results
get one line ("assumes X; does not rule out Y").

## Limitations: three buckets, never merged

1. **Author-acknowledged**: what the authors say themselves.
2. **Scope conditions**: where the result applies and where it does not, from the model or
   design itself.
3. **Reader-identified**: weaknesses that follow from the design or assumptions but that the
   authors do not raise. Mark them as your own analysis.

If the source states no limitations, say so and still fill buckets 2 and 3.

In an explainer this becomes the "What to doubt" screen: connected prose on the assumptions
most likely to fail, an evidence table with tag marks, and one sentence on how far the answer
moves if the weakest input is wrong.
